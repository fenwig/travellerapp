VEHICLES

Speed Bands

Vehicle speed is measured in bands rather than exact metres. The Speed Band Number is used in collision and other calculations. A vehicle may change speed by one band per combat round at Fast or below, or one band per minute if travelling faster. Travellers on foot use Idle. Core Rulebook p.136

Agility

A vehicle's Agility score applies as a DM to all checks made to control the vehicle. This includes all Vehicular Actions below.

All vehicle control checks = 2D + appropriate skill + Agility DM

Vehicular Actions

All of the following are Significant Actions for the driver or pilot. All skill checks use the vehicle's Agility as a DM. Core Rulebook p.138

Dogfight

A vehicle within one kilometre and within one Speed Band of another may initiate a dogfight. Both drivers make opposed skill checks. The winner chooses which fire arc their vehicle occupies on the enemy and which fire arc the enemy is in on their own vehicle. The winner gains DM+2 to all attack rolls for that round; the loser suffers DM−2. A draw means neither vehicle may attack the other unless they have a turret weapon.

If the dogfight continues into the next round, the previous winner applies the difference between the last round's checks as a positive DM to this round's opposed check.

Evasive Action

The driver makes a skill check. The Effect of the check acts as a negative DM to all attacks against the vehicle or its passengers that round. The Effect also applies as a negative DM to any attacks made from the vehicle — evasive movement disrupts outgoing fire too.

Manoeuvre

Basic vehicle control — no skill check required. The driver can avoid large or obvious obstacles, move to a destination, shift out of one enemy fire arc into another, or change which fire arc a single target is in. More extreme manoeuvres require Stunt.

Ram

Deliberately driving into another vehicle or obstacle. Requires a skill check. Ram attacks are affected by Dodge and Evasive Action as normal. The referee may grant bonuses or declare the attempt automatic if the target is very large or stationary. On success, apply Collision damage (see below).

Stunt

With a successful skill check, the driver can perform dramatic manoeuvres — standing a car on two wheels, stunning aerobatics, skimming over a sandbar. A stunt can place a target in an additional fire arc, set up a task chain, achieve up to three Manoeuvre actions in one go, or accomplish anything else the referee deems possible. The referee sets the difficulty to reflect the action attempted.

Weave

In obstacle-heavy environments (city streets, tunnels), the driver chooses a weaving score between 1 and their current Speed Band Number. They make a skill check with the weaving score as a negative DM. Failure means they have crashed into an obstacle. Success forces any pursuers to also make the same check at the same penalty or break off pursuit.

Collisions

When a vehicle collides with a Traveller, another vehicle, or an obstacle, damage is dealt based on speed. Core Rulebook p.141

Collision damage = 1D per Speed Band Number of the vehicle at impact

Damaging Vehicles

Once total damage is calculated, subtract the vehicle's Armour from it. The remaining damage is removed from the vehicle's Hull. When Hull reaches 0, the vehicle is wrecked — totally inoperable and normally beyond repair, though parts may be salvaged. Core Rulebook p.139

Damage to Hull = Weapon damage + Effect − Vehicle Armour

Vehicles have armour values for different facings. The armour used is always the facing from which the attack is made.

Light Weapons

Most personal weapons — pistols, rifles, lasers — are poorly suited to destroying vehicles. Against any weapon with less than Damage 4D or the Stun trait, a vehicle gains extra Armour equal to its Tech Level on each facing.

Example: a TL8 civilian groundcar has Armour 2. Against a rifle (3D), it effectively has Armour 10 (2 + 8). Against a PGMP (12D), it uses its normal Armour 2.

Large Vehicles

Vehicles are much easier to hit than a dodging Traveller. Attack rolls against vehicles gain DM+1, plus an additional DM+1 for every full 10 tons of the vehicle's Shipping score, to a maximum of DM+6.

Attack DM vs vehicle = +1 (base) + 1 per 10 tons Shipping (max +6)

Open vs Closed Vehicles

Open Vehicles

Grant no cover to passengers. Passengers may shoot or attack in any direction.

Closed Vehicles

Provide cover — use the vehicle's Armour as cover Protection. Passengers are limited by fire arcs and weapon mounts.

Critical Hits

A critical hit occurs when an attack roll has an Effect of 6 or higher and the attack causes damage. Roll 2D on the Critical Hits Location table and consult the Critical Hit Effects table. Core Rulebook p.140

Critical hit triggered when: attack Effect ≥ 6 and damage is caused
Severity = Effect of attack roll − 5
Sustained Damage

Even without a single high-Effect hit, a vehicle degrades under sustained fire. Every time a vehicle loses damage equal to 10% of its starting Hull, roll 2D on the Critical Hits Location table and apply a Severity 1 critical hit to that location.

Repeat Critical Hits

If a location that already has a critical hit is struck again, use the Severity of the new hit or the original plus one — whichever is higher. Once a location reaches Severity 6, it cannot take further critical hits. Instead, the vehicle takes 6D extra damage every time that location would be hit again.

Repairs

A vehicle that has suffered Hull damage may be repaired at any facility with suitable maintenance capabilities.

Average (8+) Mechanic check (1D hours + 1 per point of damage, INT or EDU)

A successful check repairs the damage. Spare parts are consumed costing Cr500 per point of Hull repaired.
