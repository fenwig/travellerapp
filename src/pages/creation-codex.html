TRAVELLER

CREATION CODEX

Overview: Building Your Character

The Character Creation tool walks you through the process of building a Traveller character step by step. You'll roll characteristics, choose careers, face mishaps and events, gain skills and benefits, and ultimately produce a fully realized adventurer ready for play.

This guide explains what to expect at each stage and highlights important things to watch out for. For detailed rules and tables, refer to the Core Rulebook, pages 8–20 (general character creation) and pages 14–15 (creation flow overview).

⚠ Group Activity: A Core Part of Traveller

Traveller creation is designed to be a GROUP activity, not a solo endeavor.

While this tool lets you create a character alone, the rulebook explicitly recommends creating characters together as a group in your first session. This serves two purposes:

  • Connections Rule: Your career might create allies, rivals, contacts, and enemies. Creating as a group lets you tie your Traveller to others in the group, building relationships before play begins.
  • Group understanding: Seeing how others' characters develop helps everyone understand the setting and each other's goals before the campaign starts.
  • Skills coverage: After everyone finishes, the group selects a final skills package to fill gaps in collective abilities.

If you're creating solo, you can always meet with your group after to establish connections and discuss how your Travellers know each other.

The Creation Journey

Step 1: Name & Species (and Origin)

Name your Traveller and choose a species: Human, Aslan or Vargr. Aslan (STR +2, DEX −2) and Vargr (STR −1, DEX +1, END −1) have characteristic modifiers that are added automatically in Step 2, and their special traits are written into your Background Notes.

Origin (Pirates of Drinax campaign): if you are playing the Drinax campaign you may also pick an origin. Standard uses the Core Rulebook rules unchanged. The three Drinax origins change your characteristics and give you a Life Events table of your own:

Drinax, Floating Palace (noble): STR −1, END −1, EDU +1, SOC +1. Suggested careers: Noble, Scholar, Navy, Entertainer.

Drinax, Wasteland (Vespexer): END +2, EDU −1, SOC −1. Suggested careers: Army, Citizen (colonist), Drifter, Rogue, Scout.

Asim: END +1, EDU −1. Suggested careers: Army, Citizen (colonist), Drifter, Entertainer, Marine.

The suggested careers are only suggestions; you may take any career. The origin's characteristic modifiers are added to your rolled characteristics in Step 2 just like species modifiers. Your Homeworld is pre-filled from the origin if you have not typed one.

Life Events: with a Drinax origin, every time a Life Event is rolled (a career event or mishap that says "Life Event", or Pre-Career Events roll 7) you roll 1D on your origin's own table instead of the Core 2D Life Events table. Results can change characteristics, add or remove Benefit rolls, give Contacts, Allies, Rivals or Enemies (often named Drinax court figures), test your PSI with DM+4, or grant automatic entry into a career. A few results, such as being enslaved by Aslan slavers on Asim, are recorded in your Background Notes for the referee to apply rather than enforced by the tool.

Core Rulebook p.13, 53-54; Pirates of Drinax Book 2, Chapter 1

Step 2: Characteristics

You determine your six characteristics (STR, DEX, END, INT, EDU, SOC). Choose one of:

  • Auto Roll for Me: the tool rolls 2D six times and you then assign the rolls to characteristics as you see fit (a Random Assign shortcut is also offered)
  • I'll Roll My Own Dice: roll physical dice, enter the six results, then assign them

Species and origin modifiers are applied automatically when you assign your rolls. You start at age 18 with the whole universe ahead of you (Colonial Upbringing can raise this).

Psionic Strength (optional, needs GM approval): before rolling characteristics you may ask to test your Psionic Strength (PSI). The tool will not roll it until you tick that your GM has approved. PSI can also be tested by chance later, with no approval needed, through the Psionic Community pre-career option and certain random events (a pre-career event or an Unusual Life Event). PSI is only rolled once per Traveller. See Psionics below.

Core Rulebook p.8-9, 228

Step 3: Background Skills & Connections

Before embarking on your career, you gain background skills equal to your EDU DM + 3 (ranging from 0 to 6 depending on your EDU score). These represent knowledge picked up during adolescence and allow you to function at a basic level in a technological society. Choose from a wide list of foundational skills like Admin, Athletics, Carouse, Drive, Electronics, Language, Mechanic, Medic, and many others—all at level 0.

Connections Rule (Core p.19): One key aspect of group creation is the Connections Rule—your Traveller's allies, contacts, rivals, and enemies create natural ties to other player characters. These connections form the basis of your party's relationships before play even begins. By creating characters together, players can establish pre-existing bonds, rivalries, or complicated relationships that enrich the campaign from the start.

A reminder of the rule appears when you reach Step 4, and the Event screens (never Mishaps) offer a connection option. Each connection must be with a different Traveller chosen from the Roster. It grants both Travellers one free skill choice, up to a maximum of two free skills per Traveller. A free skill cannot take a skill above level 3 and cannot be Jack-of-all-Trades. You pick the skills at the Finalize step.

Core Rulebook p.9, 19

Step 4: Pre-Career Education (Optional)

Before entering a career, you may choose to attend one of several pre-career education paths instead of enlisting immediately. This can be done in term one, two, or three (but only one attempt per term). Pre-career education can also be skipped entirely if you prefer to go straight into a career.

If you fail to enter or graduate, you must immediately enter a career (or be drafted if you fail qualification). From term four onwards, pre-career education is no longer available.

Each option has an entry roll, a graduation roll (rolling very high graduates with honours) and a set of graduation benefits. Rolling on the Pre-Career Events table can also happen along the way: results include a Life Event (roll 7), a chance PSI test, or a clash with a rival or enemy (a very bad roll there can land you in the Prisoner career next term).

Pre-Career Education Options

Core Rulebook Options:

University (entry EDU 6+, graduation INT 7+): Gain two skills from a wide academic range. Graduation increases both of them and EDU, and also gives DM+1 to qualify for certain careers and the ability to take a commission roll before entering a military career.

Military Academy (Army END 7+, Marines END 8+, Navy INT 8+; graduation INT 7+): Gain all service skills of the chosen military branch. Graduation increases EDU by +1 and grants automatic entry into that military career as an officer, plus bonus service skills.

Traveller Companion Options:

Colonial Upbringing (graduation INT 8+): For those raised on frontier worlds (TL8-). Gain practical skills in animals, athletics, survival, and more. Suffers DM-2 on most career entries except Rogue and Scout, and DM-1 on all promotion checks.

Merchant Academy (entry INT 9+, graduation INT 7+): For aspiring traders. Choose Business or Shipboard curriculum. Gain merchant or merchant marine skills and automatic entry at rank 1 into the Merchant or Citizen career upon graduation.

Psionic Community (entry PSI 8+, graduation PSI 6+): For those raised among psions. Your PSI is tested automatically when you choose it, then you attempt to learn talents (see Psionics). Graduation benefits include PSI +1, a talent at level 1 and Science (Psionicology) 1, but the community resents your leaving and you gain a Rival (an Enemy with honours).

School of Hard Knocks (graduation INT 7+): For those from rough backgrounds (SOC 6 or lower), who enter automatically. Gain Streetwise and combat skills, but suffer DM-2 on promotion checks in your first career.

Spacer Community: For those from orbital habitats or asteroid belts. Gain Vacc Suit and space-based skills. DM+1 on checks in the Merchant (free trader) career.

Core Rulebook p.14-16, Traveller Companion p.32-35

Step 5: Career Terms (4 Years Each)

The bulk of character creation involves serving career terms lasting four years each, even if you fail a survival roll or are forced to leave early. You can serve multiple terms in the same career, switch careers, or leave to pursue other paths. Steps 6 and 7 on the progress bar (Terms and Mustering Out) are handled inside this step.

Each term includes several phases:

Qualification: Roll to qualify for the career (using a characteristic roll, with DM-1 for each career you have already had). Success lets you join; failure means you must either submit to the draft or become a Drifter for that term. (Core p.18)

Assignment: Choose which of three possible roles you fill in this career (Army officer vs. enlisted soldier, for example). Each assignment has different survival and advancement targets.

Survival: Roll to survive the hazards of your career. If you fail, you roll on the career's Mishap table instead of the Events table, you are usually forced out of the career, and the term earns no benefit roll. Some careers are riskier than others.

Events: Roll on your career's event table. You might gain skills, allies, contacts, enemies, equipment, or face new challenges—even rivals from other careers.

Commission & Advancement: Military careers have a commission roll to become an officer. Every career has an advancement roll to rise in rank (separate from survival—you can survive but not advance). A promotion also earns an extra skill roll.

Skill Training: Gain new skills by rolling on your career's tables: Personal Development, Service Skills, the assignment's Specialist table, and Advanced Education (needs EDU 8+ or higher for some careers).

Term End: From your 4th term on, ageing is rolled first (see below). Then you choose to serve another term or leave the career.

Core Rulebook p.12-13

Careers Available

The tool offers 14 careers: Agent, Army, Citizen, Drifter, Entertainer, Marine, Merchant, Navy, Noble, Rogue, Scholar, Scout, Prisoner and Psion. Two of them work differently:

  • Prisoner cannot be chosen freely. You only enter it when a mishap, event or Life Event sends you to prison. There is no normal leave-or-stay choice: a Parole Threshold decides each term whether you are released, and mishaps never eject a Prisoner.
  • Psion needs a tested PSI before you can try to qualify (see Psionics).

Psionics

Psionic Strength (PSI) is a characteristic that is tested once per Traveller: roll 2D, subtract the terms you have served, minimum 0. It can be tested at the optional, GM-approved gate in Step 2, automatically by the Psionic Community option, or by chance through a pre-career event or an Unusual Life Event.

Talent training: once you have a PSI you may attempt to learn the five talents (Telepathy, Clairvoyance, Telekinesis, Awareness, Teleportation), one attempt each, in any order. Each attempt is 2D + your PSI DM + the talent's own DM − the number of attempts already made, against 8+. Your very first attempt at Telepathy is automatic. A talent you learn is a skill, gained at level 0.

Core Rulebook p.227-237, Traveller Companion p.32

Steps 6 & 7: Ageing, Injuries, Benefits & Mustering Out

Ageing starts at the end of your 4th term (age 34) and is rolled at the end of every term after that, before you decide whether to continue or leave. Roll 2D with a DM equal to minus your total terms served across all careers. A bad result reduces your characteristics, and you choose which ones take the loss. If STR, DEX, END, INT, EDU or SOC falls to 0 you suffer an Ageing Crisis: pay 1D × Cr10,000 for treatment or die. A Traveller who has had a crisis can no longer qualify for new careers by rolling. (Core p.48)

Injuries and medical care: a result that says "roll on the Injury table" reduces your physical characteristics. You may then pay Cr5,000 per point to restore them. If you have a career, you roll 2D + rank on its Medical Bills table to see how much is covered. The covered portion is free and the rest, or the full cost when you have no career, goes to your debt. Physical losses from a mishap or event count as well. (Core p.49)

Benefit rolls: when you leave a career you earn 1 benefit roll for each term you survived (a term where you failed survival earns none), plus a bonus for your final rank: ranks 1–2 give +1, ranks 3–4 give +2, and rank 5 or higher gives +3. For example, 4 survived terms ending at rank 3 earn 4 + 2 = 6 rolls. Some events add or remove rolls.

You choose whether each roll is on the Cash table or the Benefits table. Roll 1D (a banked bonus DM is added) and read the matching row of the career's table, up to row 7. Benefits include equipment and weapons, characteristic increases, Allies and Contacts, Ship Shares, and other career-specific rewards. Pay close attention to what you gain, since it becomes part of your character's background and resources. Cash goes straight to your cash on hand.

Pension: leaving a career after 5 or more terms grants a yearly pension, starting at Cr10,000 and rising with further terms. Scout, Rogue, Prisoner and Drifter careers earn no pension. It is added automatically. (Core p.48)

Step 8: Finalize

The last step is a review screen showing your characteristics, career history, skills, every mustering-out benefit you gained, and your cash, debt and pension. If you made connections, you pick your free connection skills first.

When you click Finish & Activate, outstanding debt (such as medical bills) is paid from your cash on hand, up to whichever is smaller, and anything left over stays as debt. The Traveller then becomes active and you are taken to the Character Record.

After activating, click the "Edit" button on your Character Record to make final adjustments:

  • Homeworld: Update where your character is from. This can be left blank if you haven't determined one yet.
  • Primary Role: Specify your character's primary function on the crew (e.g., Pilot, Medic, Navigator, Assault, Tech, Science, Steward, Admin, etc.)
  • Secondary Role: Optionally specify a secondary role your character can fill.
  • Portrait: Click "Upload Image" and pick a full-size picture of your character (a full-body image works well). Then click "Adjust Face": drag the square onto the face and use the zoom slider to frame it. The framed face becomes the portrait on your Character Record and a small thumbnail on the Roster. Anyone can click the portrait to open the full-size image in an overlay. "Remove" deletes the image.
  • On Board: On the Character List, tick the "Onboard" box to show that this character is aboard the active ship. On-board characters are now included in the crew's life support costs and living expenses, and can be given a role on the Spacecraft Record's Crew tab.

These roles describe what your Traveller does on the team—they help the group understand who fills what responsibilities during the campaign. A full Creation Log of every roll and decision is kept on the Character Record's Creation Log tab.

Tips for Success

Keys to a Successful Character

  • Know your career. Each career has unique events, mishaps, and skill tables. Read the text carefully — it tells you what each roll means.
  • Injuries are serious. If you fail a survival roll and get hurt, you'll need medical care. The creation tool will offer it, but it costs money or adds debt.
  • Terms can end early. If you fail a survival roll, mishaps can force you to leave a career. Note: if you fail survival, you do not receive a benefit roll for that term. You can still enlist elsewhere.
  • You can switch careers. If you're not happy with your first career, you can leave and join another. Each career gives you new opportunities and skills.

Understanding Skills

Granting a skill vs. increasing a skill are different things:

  • Granting at level 0: You learn the skill for the first time. If you already have the skill, granting it again at level 0 has no effect.
  • Granting at level 1: You gain expertise in the skill. If you already have level 0, this raises it to level 1. If you don't have it, you gain it at level 1.
  • Increasing a skill: You improve an existing skill. Moving from Vacc Suit 0 to Vacc Suit 1, or from level 1 to level 2, represents real progress.

Strategy tip: When choosing career options or event outcomes, prioritize actions that grant different skills rather than repeating ones you already have. If you already have Vacc Suit 0, selecting an event that grants "Vacc Suit 0" will waste the opportunity—choose something else instead.

The Power of Contacts (House Rule)

Contacts are more than just names on a sheet—they're tools for roleplay that reward your character's experiences and create narrative opportunities.

When You Gain a Contact

When an event or action grants you an ally, contact, rival, or enemy, take a moment to describe the circumstances of how you know them. This makes them real and memorable:

  • Example: Event says "You run afoul of a local bureaucrat" → enter "Local bureaucrat who opposes my work"
  • Example: While in prison, gain an ally → enter "Rival gang member I saved from a shiv attack"

These details make your contacts feel like real people with real histories.

During Gameplay: "I Know a Guy"

When your party needs outside help or has a problem to solve, you can announce "I know a guy" and pull a contact from your character sheet. You would:

  • Tell the referee which contact you're calling on
  • State whether they're an ally, contact, rival, or enemy
  • Give them a name (edit your character sheet to record it for future reference)
  • Describe how this person might help with your current situation

This mirrors the dynamic between Han Solo and Lando Calrissian in The Empire Strikes Back—a well-timed connection from your past can solve problems in unexpected ways.

For the Referee

Referees can also pull contacts from player character sheets to:

  • Create roleplay encounters (the player meets an old contact in an unexpected place)
  • Generate mission hooks (a contact calls in a favour or brings bad news)
  • Provide narrative assistance at his discretion

Contacts aren't passive list entries—they're active story drivers that breathe life into the campaign.

Monitoring Your Character

Tracking Characteristics and Skills

⚠ IMPORTANT: Carefully monitor your characteristics and skills throughout creation!

Your characteristics (STR, DEX, END, INT, EDU, SOC, and PSI) will increase or decrease based on:

  • Career assignments (some roles provide stat increases at higher ranks)
  • Career events (you might gain or lose stats)
  • Injuries (you'll lose points if you're hurt — medical care can restore them)
  • Ageing (rolled at the end of every term from your 4th, so characteristics can drop during your career)

Similarly, skills are added from:

  • Your career's training tables
  • Career events and upbringing
  • Mishaps and other special circumstances

The creation tool displays your current stats and skills in the panel at the top of each step. Check them frequently to ensure they're changing as expected. If something looks wrong, keep a note of it — you can fix it on the character sheet after creation is complete.

Discrepancies? No Problem

If you notice that a characteristic or skill didn't change when you expected it to — or changed by the wrong amount — don't panic. You can manually adjust any stat or skill on the Character Record page after creation is complete.

Simply note what should be corrected, finish creation, then edit the character sheet to fix it. It takes just a moment.
