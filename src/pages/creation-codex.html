CREATION CODEX

Overview: Building Your Character

The Character Creation tool walks you through the process of building a Traveller character step by step. You'll roll characteristics, choose careers, face mishaps and events, gain skills and benefits, and ultimately produce a fully realized adventurer ready for play.

This guide explains what to expect at each stage and highlights important things to watch out for. For detailed rules and tables, refer to the Core Rulebook, pages 8–20 (general character creation) and pages 14–15 (creation flow overview).

⚠ Group Activity: A Core Part of Traveller

Traveller creation is designed to be a GROUP activity, not a solo endeavor.

While this tool lets you create a character alone, the rulebook explicitly recommends creating characters together as a group in your first session. This serves two purposes:

  • Connections Rule: Your career might create allies, rivals, contacts, and enemies. Creating as a group lets you tie your Traveller to others in the group, building relationships before play begins.
  • Group understanding: Seeing how others' characters develop helps everyone understand the setting and each other's goals before the campaign starts.
  • Skills coverage: After everyone finishes, the group selects a final skills package to fill gaps in collective abilities.

If you're creating solo, you can always meet with your group after to establish connections and discuss how your Travellers know each other.

The Creation Journey

Step 1: Basic Information & Characteristics

Enter your character's name, species, and homeworld. These are personal details that set the stage for your character's story.

Rolling Characteristics: You'll determine your initial characteristics (STR, DEX, END, INT, EDU, SOC). You can:

  • Roll with physical dice and enter them manually, then assign the rolls to characteristics as you see fit
  • Let the tool roll for you and then assign them to the characteristics
  • Let the tool roll AND assign them automatically

You start at age 18 with the whole universe ahead of you.

Core Rulebook p.8-9

Step 2: Background Skills & Connections

Before embarking on your career, you gain background skills equal to your EDU DM + 3 (ranging from 0 to 6 depending on your EDU score). These represent knowledge picked up during adolescence and allow you to function at a basic level in a technological society. Choose from a wide list of foundational skills like Admin, Athletics, Carouse, Drive, Electronics, Language, Mechanic, Medic, and many others—all at level 0.

Connections Rule (Core p.19): One key aspect of group creation is the Connections Rule—your Traveller's allies, contacts, rivals, and enemies create natural ties to other player characters. These connections form the basis of your party's relationships before play even begins. By creating characters together, players can establish pre-existing bonds, rivalries, or complicated relationships that enrich the campaign from the start.

Core Rulebook p.9, 19

Step 3: Pre-Career Education (Optional)

Before entering a career, you may choose to attend one of several pre-career education paths instead of enlisting immediately. This can be done in term one, two, or three (but only one attempt per term). Pre-career education can also be skipped entirely if you prefer to go straight into a career.

If you fail to enter or graduate, you must immediately enter a career (or be drafted if you fail qualification). From term four onwards, pre-career education is no longer available.

Pre-Career Education Options

Core Rulebook Options:

University (EDU 6+): Gain two skills from a wide academic range and increase EDU by +1. Graduation (INT 6+) grants additional benefits including DM+1 to qualify for certain careers and the ability to take a commission roll before entering a military career.

Military Academy (Army/Marines/Navy): Gain all service skills of the chosen military branch and increase EDU by +1. Graduation (INT 7+) grants automatic entry into that military career and improved commission rolls.

Traveller Companion Options:

Colonial Upbringing: For those raised on frontier worlds (TL8-). Gain practical skills in animals, athletics, survival, and more. Suffers DM-2 on most career entries except Rogue and Scout, and DM-1 on all promotion checks.

Merchant Academy: For aspiring traders. Choose Business or Shipboard curriculum. Gain merchant or merchant marine skills and automatic entry at rank 1 into the Merchant or Citizen career upon graduation.

Psionic Community: For those with PSI 8+. Receive psionic training and testing. Automatic entry into the Psion career upon graduation, though the community may resent your departure (create a Rival or Enemy).

School of Hard Knocks: For those from rough backgrounds (SOC 6-). Automatic entry. Gain streetwise and combat skills, but suffer DM-2 on promotion checks in your first career.

Spacer Community: For those from orbital habitats or asteroid belts. Gain Vacc Suit and space-based skills. DM+1 on checks in the Merchant (free trader) career.

Core Rulebook p.14-16, Traveller Companion p.32-35

Step 4: Career Terms (4 Years Each)

The bulk of character creation involves serving career terms lasting four years each, even if you fail a survival roll or are forced to leave early. You can serve multiple terms in the same career, switch careers, or leave to pursue other paths.

Each term includes several phases:

Qualification: Roll to qualify for the career (using a characteristic roll). Success lets you join; failure means you must either submit to the draft or become a Drifter for that term. (Core p.18)

Assignment: Choose which of three possible roles you fill in this career (Army officer vs. enlisted soldier, for example). Each assignment has different survival and advancement targets.

Survival: Roll to survive the hazards of your career. You might face injury, ejection, or forced departure. Some careers are riskier than others.

Events: Roll on your career's event table. You might gain skills, allies, contacts, enemies, equipment, or face new challenges—even rivals from other careers.

Advancement: Roll to advance in rank within your career (separate from survival—you can survive but not advance).

Skill Training: Gain new skills based on your rank and assignment specialties.

Term End: Gain benefit rolls based on your rank. You'll spend these rolls to gain cash, equipment, allies, contacts, or other rewards.

Core Rulebook p.12-13

Step 5: Benefit Rolls & Mustering Out

At the end of each career term, you receive benefit rolls—a chance to gain rewards based on your rank and achievements. The number of rolls depends on your rank within that career.

These rolls are important: they build your wealth, equipment, contacts, and allies. You'll spend each benefit roll on rewards such as:

  • Cash (for purchasing equipment or establishing yourself)
  • Equipment or weapons
  • Allies and contacts
  • Ship shares (if career allows)
  • Other career-specific benefits

Pay close attention to what you're gaining—it becomes part of your character's background and resources.

Step 6: Aging & Character Completion

After all your career terms, you'll age. Aging risks begin at age 34 or after your 4th career term.

As you continue in careers beyond this point, your characteristics may decline due to the physical and mental toll of life and adventure. Each additional term after age 34 brings increased risk of injury, medical conditions, or other aging effects.

You may also experience final life events that round out your character's story.

Step 7: Group Skills Package (If Creating as a Group)

After everyone in your group has finished character creation, you collectively select a final skills package to fill any gaps in the group's collective abilities.

This ensures no critical skill areas (like piloting, medicine, or combat) are left uncovered if no individual character developed them during their career.

Core Rulebook p.18

Tips for Success

Keys to a Successful Character

  • Know your career. Each career has unique events, mishaps, and skill tables. Read the text carefully — it tells you what each roll means.
  • Injuries are serious. If you fail a survival roll and get hurt, you'll need medical care. The creation tool will offer it, but it costs money or adds debt.
  • Terms can end early. If you fail a survival roll, mishaps can force you to leave a career. Note: if you fail survival, you do not receive a benefit roll for that term. You can still enlist elsewhere.
  • You can switch careers. If you're not happy with your first career, you can leave and join another. Each career gives you new opportunities and skills.

Understanding Skills

Granting a skill vs. increasing a skill are different things:

  • Granting at level 0: You learn the skill for the first time. If you already have the skill, granting it again at level 0 has no effect.
  • Granting at level 1: You gain expertise in the skill. If you already have level 0, this raises it to level 1. If you don't have it, you gain it at level 1.
  • Increasing a skill: You improve an existing skill. Moving from Vacc Suit 0 to Vacc Suit 1, or from level 1 to level 2, represents real progress.

Strategy tip: When choosing career options or event outcomes, prioritize actions that grant different skills rather than repeating ones you already have. If you already have Vacc Suit 0, selecting an event that grants "Vacc Suit 0" will waste the opportunity—choose something else instead.

The Power of Contacts (House Rule)

Contacts are more than just names on a sheet—they're tools for roleplay that reward your character's experiences and create narrative opportunities.

When You Gain a Contact

When an event or action grants you an ally, contact, rival, or enemy, take a moment to describe the circumstances of how you know them. This makes them real and memorable:

  • Example: Event says "You run afoul of a local bureaucrat" → enter "Local bureaucrat who opposes my work"
  • Example: While in prison, gain an ally → enter "Rival gang member I saved from a shiv attack"

These details make your contacts feel like real people with real histories.

During Gameplay: "I Know a Guy"

When your party needs outside help or has a problem to solve, you can announce "I know a guy" and pull a contact from your character sheet. You would:

  • Tell the referee which contact you're calling on
  • State whether they're an ally, contact, rival, or enemy
  • Give them a name (edit your character sheet to record it for future reference)
  • Describe how this person might help with your current situation

This mirrors the dynamic between Han Solo and Lando Calrissian in The Empire Strikes Back—a well-timed connection from your past can solve problems in unexpected ways.

For the Referee

Referees can also pull contacts from player character sheets to:

  • Create roleplay encounters (the player meets an old contact in an unexpected place)
  • Generate mission hooks (a contact calls in a favour or brings bad news)
  • Provide narrative assistance at his discretion

Contacts aren't passive list entries—they're active story drivers that breathe life into the campaign.

Monitoring Your Character

Tracking Characteristics and Skills

⚠ IMPORTANT: Carefully monitor your characteristics and skills throughout creation!

Your characteristics (STR, DEX, END, INT, EDU, SOC, and PSI) will increase or decrease based on:

  • Career assignments (some roles provide stat increases at higher ranks)
  • Career events (you might gain or lose stats)
  • Injuries (you'll lose points if you're hurt — medical care can restore them)
  • Aging (after your final term, you age and lose points naturally)

Similarly, skills are added from:

  • Your career's training tables
  • Career events and upbringing
  • Mishaps and other special circumstances

The creation tool displays your current stats and skills in the panel at the top of each step. Check them frequently to ensure they're changing as expected. If something looks wrong, keep a note of it — you can fix it on the character sheet after creation is complete.

Discrepancies? No Problem

If you notice that a characteristic or skill didn't change when you expected it to — or changed by the wrong amount — don't panic. You can manually adjust any stat or skill on the Character Record page after creation is complete.

Simply note what should be corrected, finish creation, then edit the character sheet to fix it. It takes just a moment.
