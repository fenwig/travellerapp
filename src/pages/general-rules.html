GENERAL RULES

Tech Level Overview

Tech Levels (TL) measure the scientific capacity of a world and the complexity of its equipment. Most core worlds in Charted Space range between TL10 and TL13. On the fringes, Tech Levels vary wildly — isolated systems may have regressed through war or disaster, while secret research bases or Ancient relics may hold secrets beyond TL15. Core p.6–7

The Universe

No Faster-Than-Light Communications

There is no faster-than-light communication in the Third Imperium. Every message, every order, every piece of news travels at the speed of a ship. A jump-1 vessel covers one parsec in roughly one week. The fastest couriers — jump-6 ships — can move six parsecs per week, but no signal, no transmission, and no psionic power can cross interstellar space any faster than that. Core p.157

While a ship is in jumpspace it is completely and utterly cut off from the universe — a pocket dimension from which nothing can escape. It cannot communicate with the outside world, not even by psionic means.

Within a single star system, communications work normally at the speed of light — a few seconds to a few minutes depending on distance. The silence begins the moment a ship jumps.

The X-Boat Network

The Imperial Interstellar Scout Service operates a network of express boats (x-boats) — small, dedicated courier vessels stripped of everything except a jump drive, fuel, and a data store. They jump from system to system along fixed routes, transferring their message payload to the next x-boat at each waystation before immediately refuelling and jumping again.

The x-boat network is the fastest official communication channel in the Imperium. Waystations — large Scout Service installations — are maintained along the network to keep x-boats running continuously. Major banks and corporations also use the x-boat network to synchronise financial records across star systems. Core p.241

Even so, the network has limits. Routine mail — data drums and physical packages — is not entrusted to x-boats. It travels with ordinary ships at ordinary speeds.

Implications
  • News is always old. Events on a world 20 parsecs away happened months ago by the time word arrives. A war might be over before the declaration reaches the frontier.
  • Local authority is real authority. Imperial nobles and fleet commanders must make decisions without waiting for orders from Capital. A Duke on the frontier governs largely on his own judgement.
  • You can outrun your money. Electronic credits are authenticated in-system, but a traveller far from their home world may find their bank balance inaccessible — the data simply hasn't caught up. Large institutions use x-boats to synchronise records; private citizens must carry physical credits or make advance arrangements. Core p.97
  • Contacts and allies have limits. A contact can only be reached when you are in the same star system. Leaving a system means leaving them behind — unreachable until you return or they somehow receive word of where you've gone.
  • Rumour and intelligence decay. A scout report from six months ago may describe a world that has since changed government, gone to war, or been destroyed. Information is a commodity, and fresh information is worth far more than stale.

The Third Imperium

This is the age of the Third Imperium. In the year 1105, Emperor Strephon rules over an empire of eleven thousand worlds. Founded 1,105 years ago by Emperor Cleon I, the Third Imperium has expanded to encompass over 11,000 worlds, each with its own government, culture, and history.

The Imperium is not an oppressive empire crushing all dissent. It is a grand confederation providing for the common defence of its member worlds and ensuring that commerce and trade flow freely among the stars. Beyond regulating trade and providing military forces, the Imperium does little to interfere with the internal affairs of its members.

The Emperor has appointed nobles — Dukes, Counts, Barons, and Knights — to aid his rule, carrying the Emperor's word and enforcing Imperial law across the stars.

A Short History

The Vilani & the Ziru Sirka

In −3224 (Earth year 2092), humanity reached the stars — only to find more humans. For hundreds of parsecs toward the galactic core, human-inhabited worlds stretched in every direction. These worlds had been united under the rule of the Vilani in the Ziru Sirka (Grand Imperium of the Stars) for over 1,500 years. But that long rule had a price: cultural rigidity and stagnation.

The Solomani & the Rule of Man

The dynamic Terrans — later called Solomani — exploited Vilani stagnation in a series of interstellar wars that brought down the Ziru Sirka. In its place they erected the Rule of Man, uniting Vilani and Solomani under a single rule. But even Solomani dynamism could not arrest the decline. Within 400 years, civil wars and economic collapse caused the Rule of Man to fall.

The Long Night

With the collapse, both Vilani and Solomani worlds slid into the Long Night — 1,500 years of isolation, regression, and darkness as interstellar trade and communication died away.

Cleon I & the Third Imperium

As the Long Night began to recede, worlds started reaching out to the stars again. On the world of Sylea, a man named Cleon Zhunastu dreamed of restoring the glory of the old empires. In a thirty-year campaign he transformed the Sylean Federation into the foundation of something new. In the 654th year of the Sylean Federation, Cleon and the Grand Senate proclaimed the Third Imperium, with Cleon Zhunastu crowned Emperor Cleon I.

The Ancients & The Human Question

Throughout history, one question has remained unanswered: where did humanity come from? Who placed humans among the stars — and why?

Archaeological evidence has revealed several things:

  • Genetic analysis indicates humanity was scattered across the stars approximately 300,000 years ago
  • Ruins found on many human homeworlds were built by a non-human race over 300,000 years ago
  • This race destroyed itself in a 10,000-year war, leaving humanity to fend for itself
  • The best candidate for the original human homeworld is Terra — the Solomani homeworld

This race is known as the Ancients. It is believed they also took members of the canis genus (dogs, wolves) and uplifted them to sentience — these became the Vargr. Why the Ancients chose to scatter humanity, and why they destroyed themselves, remains unknown.

Alien Races

In addition to scattered descendants of humanity, alien races are found throughout the stars. They fall into two categories:

Major Races

Discovered the jump drive independently. Includes: Humanity (Solomani of Terra, Vilani of Vland, Zhodani of Zhdant), the Aslan, the Vargr, the Hiver, the K'Kree, and the Droyne.

Minor Races

Learned the secret of the jump drive from another race rather than discovering it independently. Numerous minor races exist throughout Charted Space.

Core Mechanic

Making a Check
2D + Skill Level + Characteristic DM + other DMs ≥ Target Number

Roll 2D and add your skill level and the relevant characteristic DM. If the total meets or exceeds the target number, you succeed. The referee should only call for checks when the Travellers are in danger, the task is especially difficult or hazardous, they are under time pressure, or success and failure are both interesting outcomes.

If no difficulty is specified, assume the task is Average (8+).

Boon, Bane & Effect

Boon

Roll an extra die and discard the lowest. Granted when a Traveller has helpful circumstances — good tools, assistance, favourable conditions.

Bane

Roll an extra die and discard the highest. Applied when hindered — poor tools, darkness, distractions, adverse conditions.

A Boon and a Bane on the same check cancel out. Multiple Boons or Banes still only count as one.

Effect

Effect = Total rolled − Target Number. Measures how well (or badly) a Traveller performed. Used to calculate combat damage and task chain bonuses.

EffectResultMeaning
−6 or lessExceptional FailureEverything that can go wrong does. Devices damaged, situations made worse.
−2 to −5Average FailureThe task simply fails.
−1Marginal FailureAlmost succeeded. Referee may allow scraping a success with a significant cost.
0Marginal SuccessBarely succeeds — may need to accept a condition or complication.
1 to 5Average SuccessThe task succeeds normally.
6+Exceptional SuccessSucceeds in an impressive and elegant fashion.

Special Check Types

Opposed Checks

When two characters directly oppose each other, both make their checks normally. The winner is the one with the highest Effect. On a draw, neither gains an advantage — they may try again or events overtake them.

Multiple Tasks

Doing two or more things at once raises the difficulty of each task by one level for every extra task being performed simultaneously.

Timeframes

Each task has a random timeframe of 1–6 time units. The referee sets the increment based on the nature of the task.

Environmental Dangers

Full tables: Core Rulebook p.80–82.

Disease & Poison

Make an END check at the listed difficulty. On failure, take the listed damage and repeat after the interval. p.80

Falling

1D damage per 2 metres fallen on a 1G world. A successful Athletics check reduces the effective distance by the Effect. Armour does not protect against falling damage. p.80

Fatigue

Fatigued Travellers suffer DM−2 to all checks until they rest. p.80

Radiation

Measured in rads — cumulative and permanent until treated. Effects range from nausea (51–150 rads) to death (800+ rads). p.81

Suffocation

Without life support: 1D damage per minute. Without any air: 1D damage per round. p.81

Vacuum

Cumulative damage each round — 1D in round 1, 2D in round 2, 3D in round 3, and so on. p.81

Temperature

Extreme heat and cold deal damage to END — from 1D/hour in arctic to 3D/round in an inferno. p.82

Gravity

High gravity (1.4G+): DM−1 to all checks until acclimatised. Low/Zero-G: DM−1 to all physical checks; firing recoil weapons requires Athletics (dexterity) Average (8+). p.80

Medical Treatment

First Aid
Medic (EDU) check — restores characteristic points equal to Effect (minimum 1)

Must be initiated within one minute of the injury. Requires at least a basic medikit. Can only be successfully applied once per injury.

Medical Care
3 + END DM + Medic skill = characteristic points restored per day

Available when only one or two physical characteristics are damaged. Requires a hospital or sickbay and full bed rest.

Surgery

Required when all three physical characteristics are damaged. Restores points equal to the Effect of a Medic (EDU) check.

Warning: a failed surgery check causes the patient to lose an additional 3 + Effect characteristic points.

Augmentation & Medical Care

All Medic checks suffer a negative DM equal to the difference in Tech Level between the medical facility and the Traveller's highest relevant implant.

Natural Healing

Normal Rest
1D + END DM per day of full rest
After Surgery
END DM only per day of rest

If END DM is negative, the Traveller continues to deteriorate without medical care.

Unconsciousness

Make an END check after every minute. Success means the Traveller regains consciousness. Each failed check grants a cumulative DM+1 to the next attempt.

Mental Characteristics

INT and EDU damage heals at 1 point per characteristic per day unless otherwise specified.
